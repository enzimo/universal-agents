# Goal Contract, Procedure Set, Team, and Plan

- Collect the governing procedure set

## Establish the Goal Contract

Capture:

- desired outcome and value
- sponsor, users, stakeholders, and decision authorities
- scope, non-goals, hard constraints, and preferences
- accepted and provisional success criteria, required evidence, and evaluators
- failure, rollback, partial-delivery, stop, and acceptance conditions
- limits of authority, risk tolerance, budget, and deadline
- assumptions, unknowns, inputs, dependencies, and first safe action
- objective lifecycle authority and health-review cadence

Classify the demand as one task, task plus plan, or durable objective. Avoid
creating an objective for a simple task. Avoid hiding a long-running or
recurring responsibility inside one expanding task.

Declare the goal ready enough only when a plausible outcome exists, the next
action respects known constraints and authority, the action creates value or
information, its result is observable, and the required procedure coverage for
that action exists.

When the final definition of done is incomplete, record the best current
version instead of leaving completion implicit. Mark each criterion accepted or
provisional. Define its measurement or named evaluator, evidence freshness,
limitations of any proxy, and discovery or review point. Permit a bounded Task
to finish against its local completion criteria while the Objective remains
open. Require the designated human or acceptance authority to approve material
changes to accepted outcome, quality, risk, cost, scope, or completion criteria.

## Collect the Governing Procedure Set

Identify every domain materially touched by the goal. Include technical,
operational, safety, security, legal, financial, research, organizational, and
other relevant domains.

For each applicable procedure, standard, policy, method, or review rule,
record:

| Field | Meaning |
| --- | --- |
| Name | Stable procedure identity |
| Domain | Specialty or control area governed |
| Source and owner | Authoritative origin and decision authority |
| Version / effective date | Current revision and freshness basis |
| Scope | Work, environment, role, and conditions covered |
| Precedence | Relationship to law, policy, goal, and other procedures |
| Applicability | Why the procedure applies to this objective or task |
| Required evidence | Proof of conformance or approved deviation |
| Gaps / conflicts | Missing, contradictory, or unsuitable provisions |
| Review triggers | Events requiring reassessment |

Prefer current authoritative primary sources. Use a domain specialist when
interpretation or applicability requires expertise. Treat a missing or unclear
material procedure as an unknown; do not replace it silently with generic
OODA guidance.

Create a provisional team-owned procedure only when authorized. Bound its
scope, assumptions, validation, stop conditions, owner, version, and review
date. Request approval before deviating from an externally controlled
procedure.

Reassess procedures after milestones, failures, recovery, long pauses,
environment or policy changes, new domains, repeated friction, and evidence
that contradicts an assumed effect. Propagate approved revisions and recheck
work produced under invalidated procedures.

## Reduce Ambiguity

Classify ambiguity as outcome, scope, success, constraint, priority, fact,
feasibility, authority, timing, dependency, terminology, or procedure.

For each material ambiguity:

1. State the unknown and the decision it can change.
2. Rank its impact and urgency.
3. Choose discovery, experiment, reversible default, domain procedure, or
   human decision as the resolution method.
4. Assign an owner, budget, stop condition, and evidence requirement.
5. Record the result and update dependent work.

Ask the human when the ambiguity represents a human-owned value tradeoff,
constraint change, risk acceptance, protected action, or materially different
outcome. Continue only work valid across every plausible answer while waiting.

## Shape the Team

Use one agent by default. Add roles only for:

- distinct domain expertise
- independent high-consequence review
- parallel independent discovery
- isolation of recovery or untrusted context
- clear capacity gains on independent workstreams

Define one accountable owner, bounded outcome, relevant procedures, input
packet, deliverable interface, acceptance evidence, resource bound, progress
cadence, and escalation condition for every delegation. Avoid overlapping
ownership and repeated handoffs.

Match the delegated planning view to the owner's level:

- Give the Objective or Plan owner the accepted outcome, whole-Plan tradeoffs,
  cross-subplan dependencies, actionable frontier, and human decision bounds.
- Give a subplan owner the parent outcome and constraints, assigned subgoal,
  sibling interfaces, local progress definition, and upward decision triggers.
- Give a Task owner the bounded result, parent contribution, inputs,
  constraints, expected evidence, local method freedom, and blocker path.

Provide enough parent context to prevent local optimization against the larger
outcome. Avoid burdening a Task owner with unrelated Plan detail.

Choose sequential work for shared-state or dependency-heavy tasks. Choose
parallel work only when workstreams are independent and fan-in criteria are
explicit. Use independent review rather than majority voting for consequential
disagreement.

## Build the Living Plan

Plan backward from success evidence:

1. Define required evidence and deliverables.
2. Define milestones and decision gates.
3. Map procedure obligations, dependencies, unknowns, risks, and integration.
4. Detail only the committed horizon.
5. Keep later work at forecast or discovery level.

Include for every material plan item:

- title, objective, and rationale
- owner and dependencies
- entry conditions and governing procedures
- current actionability and its evidence
- local definition of progress and contribution to the parent
- deliverable, completion criteria, and verification
- estimate range, confidence, and assumptions
- risk, blocker behavior, stop or replan trigger, checkpoint, and downstream
  effect

Treat a plan item as complete only when its result and evidence are integrated,
not when its owner merely reports activity.

## Maintain the Actionable Frontier

Classify each material Plan branch independently:

| State | Use when | Required treatment |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Actionable now | Entry conditions hold and a bounded next action is authorized | Execute or schedule it and name the expected evidence |
| Conditional | A known event, observation, or decision determines relevance | Preserve the condition and prepare only work useful across plausible outcomes |
| Blocked | A credible route remains but a specific obstacle prevents its next necessary action | Own the blocker, seek bounded unblocking or an alternative, and set a review trigger |
| Currently unachievable | No credible route exists within current authority, capability, evidence, time, or resource bounds | Record the limiting bounds and escalate deferment, changed constraints, a changed outcome, or termination |
| Completed or superseded | The result is integrated or no longer needed | Preserve reusable evidence and reconcile dependent work |

Treat actionability separately from planning horizon. Permit a later discovery
item to be actionable while a near-term delivery is blocked. Keep useful
independent branches moving, but do not invent work that lacks an accepted
contribution.

For every Objective, Plan, subplan, and Task, define what making progress
entails:

- baseline or prior evidenced state
- expected evidence-bearing change
- contribution to its parent outcome gap
- evidence source and integration point
- useful partial result
- checkpoint or attempt bound for no-delta reorientation

Use Objective progress for evidenced movement toward accepted success or for a
material value, feasibility, or acceptance decision. Use subplan progress for
integrated milestones, reduced risk or uncertainty, satisfied dependencies, or
enabled decisions. Use Task progress for movement toward its bounded local
result. Reject activity that has no credible parent contribution.

Record a blocker with its affected branch, parent contribution, exact limiting
condition, owner, evidence, condition needed to proceed, affected dependencies,
independent work, prior bounded attempts, alternatives, and next review trigger.
Treat "currently unachievable" as relative to current bounds and revisit it only
on its trigger.

## Control Plan Changes

Update the plan after new evidence, goal or procedure change, missed milestone,
recovery, estimate variance, dependency change, or objective-health decision.
Record what changed, why, affected work, invalid evidence, forecast impact, and
the next committed action.

Refine subgoals as evidence improves. Split items that need different owners,
dependencies, evidence, or failure handling; merge distinctions that no longer
help; and replace invalidated hypotheses. Preserve every item's link to the
parent outcome gap. Treat newly visible adjacent work as a separate candidate
until scope authority accepts it.

Respect planning authority. Permit a Task owner to change method inside the
Task contract. Permit a subplan owner to revise its route inside delegated
interfaces and bounds. Require the Plan owner to reconcile cross-subplan
changes. Escalate changes to accepted outcomes, success criteria, material
risk, resource bounds, or required results to the designated higher-order agent
or human.

When escalation is required, provide current evidence, the failed or blocked
route, alternatives considered, the recommended change, effects on criteria
and sibling work, and safe progress that can continue while awaiting the
decision. Do not silently convert a local obstacle into a parent-scope change.

Stop or steer stale tasks before they create incompatible effects. Preserve
valid results. Classify discovered scope as required, blocking risk, optional
improvement, or separate future objective.
